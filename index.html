01 / THE PERSON BEHIND THE WORK

About Me

My journey into immersive technology started in the world of circuits and oscilloscopes. I trained as an Electrical Engineer at the Chittagong University of Engineering and Technology (CUET), where I was captivated by the logic of systems and the elegance of precision engineering. But something was missing—until I encountered code that could respond, adapt, and interact.

The turning point came when I first experienced Virtual Reality. It wasn’t just the headset or visuals—it was the feeling of presence, the sense that you could think, act, and feel inside a digital world. That moment sparked a profound shift: from hardware to human-centered computing, from circuits to cognition. I realized that I wanted to design systems that didn't just work—but communicated, responded, and understood.

After graduation, I joined Walton Digi-Tech Industries Limited as a Quality Control Engineer in the Cellphone Quality Assurance Team. I gained hands-on experience in testing and validating complex products—but the deeper takeaway was how tightly technology and human perception are intertwined. That insight fueled my move into Computer Science, where interaction, data, and behavior became the core of my work.

Today, I’m a researcher and VR developer working at the intersection of Virtual Reality (VR), Human-Computer Interaction (HCI), and Data Science. My research focuses on cognitive load, motion precision, and usability in immersive environments—using Unity, Python, and machine learning to turn behavior into insight and feedback into design.

I recently completed my Master of Science in Computer Science and a Graduate Certificate in Data Science at the University of Arkansas at Little Rock. I’m now pursuing a Ph.D. in Computer Science at the same institution, building intelligent, privacy-aware simulation systems for training, performance, and human-machine collaboration.

I believe that immersive technologies are not just tools—they are environments for learning, decision-making, and connection. My goal is to design adaptive, inclusive virtual worlds where humans and systems work in sync, turning complexity into clarity.
